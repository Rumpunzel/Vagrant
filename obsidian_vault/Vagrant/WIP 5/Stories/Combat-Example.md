As mentioned in the beginning of `Violence.md`, if one character aims to do harm, the target may either flee or fight back. As such, each exchange is technically a decision between fight for flight. Fleeing doesn't necessarily cost anything; instead of inflicting an injury, you escape the fight (although, logically, you would still inflict an injury when fleeing while wielding a weapon).
![Combat Selection](image.bmp)

Technically, I would say the aggressor picks stance and breath dice to roll first. In the case of the lurker ambush, the descriptive text could change based on the decision made. But I think it makes the most sence to roll all dice at the same time, especially as the threat number is secret until the exchange gets resolved anyway.

# Unarmed Exchange

1. Lurker threatens player
2. Player decides to fight back
3. Player has 7 Guts, all his breath dice; Lurker has 5 Guts and 3d8 breath dice (I think it would make sense to give most monsters simplified breath dice, to make running at the table for the GM easier)
4. Player rolls: [1, 1, 2, 2, 7]; Lurker rolls [3, 4, 5]; no breath dice are lost; Lurker suffers a severity 2 injury, which reduces all his attributes by 2, so now he has 3 Guts
5. Exchange over, either party could now flee, both decide to fight on
6. Player rolls: [2, 2, 4, 7, 9]; Lurker rolls [3, 4, 4]; Player loses the d12 breath die, which rolled the 9; Lurker loses both dice, which came up 4, as his Guts is now 3; Lurker suffers a severity 5 injury, which reduces all his attributes by 5, so now he has below 0 Guts, so he will lose his one remainig breath die in the next exchange either way. He could flee in the next exchange to at least try to get away. If he stays to fight, he will be incapacitated after that exchange.

# Armed Exchange

Very similar to the above example, but each side also rolls a weapon die.
But before each exchange, each side also chooses the attribute with which to engage
In step 4: Player chooses to engage with Guts, as before, and rolls a 3 on his d10 weapon die; Lurker also engages with Guts rolls a 2 on his d8 weapon die; Player suffers a severity 2 injury; Lurker suffers a severity 5 injury (3 + 2)
In step 6: Player will also lose the die, which came up 7 in addition to the one which came up 9, as his Guts is now 5; Lurker will also lose the die, which came up 3 and will be out of breath after this exchange, which means it can no longer threated or physically resist the Player; Player chooses to engage with Blood now (same score as Guts for simplicity); Lurker still engages with Guts; Player weapon die comes up 10 and hence unusable this exchange; Lurker weapon die comes up 1; Player does not suffer an injury, because he enganged with Blood and decreased the injury he suffers instead of increasing the injury inflicted with their weapon; Lurker does not suffer an injury
